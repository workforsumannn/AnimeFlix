Playstyle: One-Tap adds +8 General / +10 Red Dot. Rusher adds +12 General but drops Sniper by 12. Full Spray lowers everything slightly and enlarges the Fire Button by 4%. Sniper drops General by 12 and raises Sniper scope by 24.

Refresh rate: 90 Hz adds +3 General, 120 Hz adds +6, 144 Hz adds +8.

BGMI: Gyro starts at 380 (No Scope) and falls per scope — 360 / 330 / 275 / 210 / 140 / 80. RAM, FPS, finger count and gyro style add or subtract points. Non-Gyro sets gyro to 0 and boosts ADS instead.

What it can't know: screen size, finger speed, grip and practice time. Use the result as a starting point — change one value at a time by ~5 until your crosshair stops overshooting.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Can using this calculator get my account banned?

No. UltimateSensi Pro only suggests numbers you type into the game's own settings menu. It doesn't modify files, inject scripts or contact game servers. Adjusting your own sensitivity is a normal part of both games.

How do RAM and refresh rate affect sensitivity?

Budget phones drop more frames, so they start higher (General 190–196 on 2–3 GB) and step down for stronger phones (158 on 12 GB+). On 90–144 Hz screens we add 3–8 points because smoother motion makes fast drags easier to control.

What's the best Fire Button size for one-tap headshots?

For most devices, 42%–48% in the lower-right third works well — it leaves room to drag upward. Test a few sizes in Training Mode and keep the most consistent one.

Does this require root or GFX tools?

No. Zero root, zero external config files, zero third-party GFX modifications. You only change settings inside the game menus.

How often should I change my sensitivity?

Stick with one preset for at least 5–7 days in Training Ground and Clash Squad to build muscle memory before making fine adjustments.
